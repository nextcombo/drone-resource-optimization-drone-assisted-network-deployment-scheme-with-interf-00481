# [Drone Resource Optimization] Drone-Assisted Network Deployment Scheme with Interference Restrictions Based on Particle Swarm Optimization Algorithm [with MATLAB Code]

This paper investigates the deployment issues of using unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) as access points to provide wireless services in green spaces. Under the actual air-to-ground (ATG) channel model, three basic deployment designs are studied: the minimum number of UAVs, their optimal locations, and the optimal power allocation. Since these three design objectives are interrelated, a Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)-based approach is proposed, integrating a balanced signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) power allocation. By leveraging the closed-form expression for optimizing the number of UAVs based on SINR and the resulting SINR, the proposed PSO-based scheme iteratively optimizes both the number of UAVs and their locations until each user's SINR reaches the required minimum value. To improve the convergence performance of this scheme, two initial estimation methods for the minimum number of UAVs are designed: first analyzing system and rate capacity, followed by initializing UAV positions using K-means clustering technology. Finally, a power fine-tuning scheme is developed to further reduce total transmission power. Extensive simulation results confirm the good performance of the proposed scheme.
Introduction: Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) have been recognized as one of the most promising technologies for revolutionizing future wireless network architectures. With their high mobility, UAVs can be deployed as mobile access points or relay nodes to respond to the surge in real-time data traffic. Therefore, UAV-assisted networks can provide more comprehensive network coverage through flexible network planning and deployment. For example, when ground communication infrastructure is damaged by natural disasters such as earthquakes, UAV-assisted networks can serve as airborne base stations for emergency service restoration. However, to realize the advantages promised by UAVs in practice, extensive research work is needed.
Firstly, optimizing the deployment location of drones to enhance their coverage and throughput is crucial. Besides its high dimensionality, the optimization problem generally becomes NP-hard. After establishing a three-dimensional (3D) air-to-ground (ATG) channel model, the optimal height is derived as a function of maximum allowable path loss and environmental parameters. Based on the ATG channel model, a scheme has been developed to determine the 3D position of low-altitude quadrotor drones using the concept of circular packing theory to maximize joint coverage area and lifespan. However, this approach assumes non-overlapping coverage, which is not applicable in real-world drone deployment scenarios. Additionally, heuristic algorithms have been proposed to optimize the deployment of high-altitude drones. For example, considering a drone-assisted heterogeneous network for public safety communication, an exhaustive search over all feasible deployment locations was used to optimize drone deployment. In contrast, focusing on single-drone scenarios. More specifically, minimizing the total path loss between the drone and all users by proposing a dynamic programming method for drone trajectory planning that optimizes drone speed and altitude. Finally, transforming drone trajectory design into a mixed integer non-convex optimization problem to maximize the minimum throughput for all ground users.
Secondly, energy consumption is also a challenging issue in drone-assisted wireless networks. Due to the power limitations of drones driven by batteries, there are strict power constraints for single drone networks. For ground activity detection, a scheme has been designed to optimize the energy consumption of drones. Meanwhile, research has been conducted on drones with energy collection capabilities. In contrast, the study focuses on wireless networks equipped with multiple drones, with an emphasis on their energy efficiency and uplink transmission reliability. Furthermore, by assuming all drones operate in completely orthogonal frequency channels, interference-free scenarios have been considered. However, this issue has not been fully explored in more general interference-limited environments where multiple drones operate in non-orthogonal frequency channels. It is evident that the amount of interference between drones generally increases with the increase in the number of drones. Therefore, deploying the minimum number of drones to reduce costs and better manage interference is advisable.
In this paper, based on previous work, we focus on minimizing the number of deployed drones while optimizing their deployment locations and transmission powers under conditions that satisfy all users' minimum SINR values. This task is highly challenging as it involves three highly coupled design objectives: the minimum number of drones, their optimal deployment locations, and their optimal transmission powers.
To address this problem, we propose optimizing both drone locations and their transmission powers jointly while fixing the number of drones first. If the obtained SINR does not meet the requirements, we increase the number of drones deployed. This process is repeated until the required SINR is met, with joint optimization of locations and transmission powers in each iteration. Unlike existing particle swarm optimization (PSO)-based algorithms, our algorithm explicitly uses SINR as the performance metric for evaluating the performance of each drone location, allowing the proposed solution to calculate closed-form optimal transmission powers and thereby optimal SINR directly, whereas existing algorithms use path loss as the performance metric to guide drone position updates.

Conclusion In this paper, we have developed a drone deployment scheme based on Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) for constrained interference-limited wireless networks. By optimizing three fundamental deployment issues: the minimum number of drones, their optimal deployment locations, and the optimal power allocation, we satisfy the given SINR requirements. To derive the optimal positions and power allocations, we employ the SINR balanced power allocation method in each PSO iteration. Additionally, since the computational complexity of the proposed PSO-based scheme largely depends on the initial estimation of the number of drones, we propose two schemes to accurately estimate the initial value of the number of drones by analyzing network and rate capacity before the start of the PSO iterations. Furthermore, we introduce an initialization algorithm based on K-means clustering for initializing the drone positions in the first PSO iteration. Finally, we develop a power fine-tuning scheme to further reduce the total transmission power. Extensive simulation results validate the outstanding performance of the proposed PSO-based scheme.
In future work, we will consider relaxing some of the strict assumptions used in deriving the proposed scheme. For example, we will extend our work to scenarios where user locations are not fully known and users are distributed across different heights. Additionally, we will consider more realistic implementation constraints such as random gusts and wind, as well as network synchronization. As shown, random gusts can cause UE - drone beam misalignment, which in turn can degrade system performance. Moreover, synchronization issues among multiple drones can lead to reduced network capacity. We will explore more robust deployment schemes to address these practical implementation challenges.
